What drives me Service

Serve the person on the other end.

I got into accessibility because I like being friendly and reliable, an advocate for people who need it. Every interface has a person on the other end; some of those people use a screen reader, a keyboard, or a slow connection. One exception: A/B test variants shouldn't fix an inaccessible control unless they're testing accessibility. So results stay honest and protect the data.

Performant and accessible is the floor. Above it, I want the work to be beautiful enough to delight yet practical enough to feel intuitive.

How I work Full spectrum

Both, on purpose.

I push boundaries when a project needs it, but I'm happy playing it safe when it serves the audience better.

Push.

I reworked my team's code review skills into a shared, accessibility-aware agentic workspace that almost a dozen engineers rely on.

Safe.

For Dell's XPS relaunch I learned GSAP mid-build so an agency's animations could ship on time. GSAP kept their animation and functionality intact. Rebuilding it in vanilla JavaScript and advanced CSS would have been more performant, but riskier on a deadline.

On cmu.edu, I wove the wordmark square above and behind page sections on scroll. Leadership kept the square but cut the weave. Though disappointed, I could see why.
